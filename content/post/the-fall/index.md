I think that there is something more going on.
Consider the fact that very few fundamentalists think that the stars are fixed in a firmament even though that really was the consensus scientific view at the time the various Biblical passages saying so were written. Fundamentalists will take the firmament thing as metaphorical even though historical context tells us that this that really is what people believed.
The only place we see large scale rejection of the science because it conflicts with the Bible is around the Creation story. Sure you fill find the occasional Fundamentalist who rejects the idea that the moon reflects light or that the starts aren't fixed in a firmament, but those people are rare. While the rejection of Natural Section is extremely widespread.
I believe I have an explanation for why Natural Selection (and supporting things about the age of the Earth) is the science that they choose to reject.
In lots of cultures, creation stories (whether symbolic or literal) are taken to say something essential about who we are and our place in relationship with the creator(s). People often don't like what they believe Natural Selection says about who we are. Both supporters and opponents of evolution believed that it says some nasty things about who we are. Some of those beliefs are mistaken, but they were very widely held at the time and still very often held today. When we say something like "Jeffrey Dahlmer is an animal", we aren't being kind to him.
It is important to remember that early 20th century Creationists were progressives who were rejected "Social Darwinism". Consider William Jennings Bryant as a leading example of this. Today, the anti-evolution crowd is larger very conservative, but it wasn't always that way. And today it is also very common among American Black Christians, whose very recent ancestors have been victims of "science-based" eugenics movements. Sure the science behind racism was wrong, but Darwinism was used to justify a lot of really terrible things. Although some early Darwinists pointed out that cooperation can play as large a role as competition in evolutionary success, the focus was on competition until cooperation was revived against with the advent of Sociobiology in the 1970s. (Sociobiology really is about cooperation, and mostly focused on insects, but it got a really bad rap because people who never read any of it thought that it was Social Darwinism.)
But even for those who like some of those perceived nasty bits are uncomfortable with us just being another kind of animal for reasons having to do with souls, moral responsibility and our relationship with God. (As an aside, my take on the story of the Fall is that it is exactly the idea that knowledge of good and evil burdens us with shame and moral responsibility, but is essential to use being human.)
